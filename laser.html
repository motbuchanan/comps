CHAPTER 01

Know What Kind of Laser You Have

The brand name matters less than the source, work area, safety design, and material.

Tap your laser type
Wattage is not a score. Two 40-watt machines can need completely different settings. Wavelength, spot size, focus, lens, motion system, air assist, and the material itself all matter more than the number on the box.
Learn these controls — tap each
Beginner rule: copy the process, not the number. A manufacturer setting is a starting range. Your verified test result is the production setting.

Good work begins before the beam turns on.

Clean files reduce burn time and prevent rejected orders.

CHAPTER 07

Finish, Inspect, and Package Like a Seller

Customers judge the finished piece, not how hard the machine worked.

The seven-point quality check
Create a golden sample. Keep your best finished piece or a clearly lit reference photo, and compare every batch to it. It stops quality from slowly drifting when you get busy or switch suppliers.
When to remake: if the defect is obvious, changes personalization, weakens the item, or makes the work look careless — remake it. Do not ship a defect and hope the buyer overlooks it. The correct engraving on the wrong blank is still a wrong order.

Price for Profit Without Fooling Yourself

The blank is only one part of the cost. Price the entire job.

The example is an illustration, not a recommended market price. Replace every number with your own costs and local demand. Platform fees change — verify before listing.

Three pricing traps
"The laser did it in five minutes." Setup, design, cleaning, messaging, testing, and packaging still took time.
"The blank only cost $1." A low material cost does not make labor and overhead free.
"Someone online sells it for less." Their materials, quality, labor target, and business model may be completely different.
A healthy price does three things: covers the real cost, pays for the work, and leaves enough profit to make the next order worth accepting.
